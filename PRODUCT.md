# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Hackathon judges at Since AI 2026, watching a two-minute demo video and possibly opening the app themselves. They know data
protection in general but not this tool. They have seconds per screen, so each screen has to show what it is for, what to do
next and what the system decided, before any explanation is read. Behind them sits the real user the tool is built for: a
privacy officer at a hospital or company deciding whether a dataset may go to a researcher, vendor or the public.

## Product Purpose

PETs Transformer is a privacy release gate. A person picks a dataset and says who will receive it; the engine labels every
column, protects the data with the right technique for that recipient, measures that the recipient's questions still work,
and then releases, holds for review, or blocks. Success is a judge understanding, within one screen, that the data was
protected, how useful it still is, and why the gate decided what it did.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, trustworthy. The interface speaks like a careful auditor: plain sentences, precise numbers, no hype. Confidence
comes from evidence shown clearly, not from decoration. A verdict should feel earned and believable.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI SaaS: purple or blue gradients, glass cards, glowing blobs, sparkle icons for "AI".
- Playful consumer apps: cartoon illustrations, bouncy motion, emoji.
- Hacker terminals: neon on black, monospace everywhere, matrix effects.

## Design Principles

1. Verdict first. Every screen leads with the decision or the next action; detail sits one step below.
2. Show the evidence. Numbers come with their meaning and their comparison, so trust is earned, not asserted.
3. Practise what it preaches. A privacy tool must look careful: nothing cluttered, nothing hidden, nothing exaggerated.
4. One path at a time. The four-step flow is the spine; each step has one primary action.
5. Honest states. Loading, empty, held and blocked states say exactly what happened and what to do.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast of at least 4.5:1, visible focus on every control, full keyboard use, screen-reader labels for icons
and status, and no meaning carried by colour alone. Motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`. Layouts work from phone to
ultra-wide.
